About these numbers: tier classification follows a two-axis rule — decided-audience size combined with length-adjusted retention — rather than a single size cutoff, with one override: past roughly 1,000,000 decided viewers, the tool assigns Mainstream regardless of retention, since a completed audience that large likely exceeds most of MAL's realistic active-tracker base on its own. Retention is reported with a Wilson score interval (95%); for a title with hundreds of thousands of decided viewers, that interval is razor-tight, meaning the retention figure itself is close to exact. Most of the real uncertainty here comes from the weights and multipliers applied afterward, not from the retention measurement. Length-adjusted retention normalizes to a 12-episode reference by assuming a constant per-episode drop rate — a real, named technique (geometric survival normalization), though the constant-hazard assumption is a simplification. Real attrition tends to be front-loaded, with most drop-off happening in the first few episodes, so for very long runs this adjustment likely overstates true stickiness; treat it as directional rather than exact. The tier thresholds (85%/50% retention, 80K/1M audience size) are still round-number judgment calls rather than values fit to real data, since no published dataset would let you fit them properly.

How the range is built: every uncertain factor above is treated as a Low–Likely–High triangular distribution rather than a single fixed number. The calculator draws 6,000 random combinations from those distributions, runs the full pipeline for each, and reports the median plus the middle band as your range. Raw MAL counts are treated as exact — only the assumptions carry uncertainty, not the numbers you looked up. Parameters are sampled independently, which slightly understates the true uncertainty if some of these actually move together in reality. And the range is only as honest as the Low/High you set — narrowing them will always shrink the interval, whether or not that's justified.
